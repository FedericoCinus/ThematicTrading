TASK. For one Bloomberg headline and its date, label (1) the named entities and (2) at most ONE economic relation between two actors.

1 · ENTITIES
List every organisation (ORG) and every person (PERSON) named in the headline, as written.
- ORG: companies, banks, funds, brokers, regulators and government agencies, courts, universities, news outlets. PERSON: named people.
- Not entities: countries, cities, regions (Russia, UK, NYC); products, drugs, apps, projects; securities and instruments (bonds, bills, AT1s, shares); indices; currencies; generic groups (chipmakers, investors, holders, banks).
- Write the surface form without possessive ('s / ’s): "Google’s AI Chatbot" -> Google. Keep multiword names whole ("Pioneer Natural Resources", "Goldman Sachs").
- A country adjective + company is the company only: "Indonesia’s PP" -> PP; "Switzerland’s Terra Quantum" -> Terra Quantum.
- Named sources are entities too ("..., Nikkei Says" -> Nikkei; "...: FAZ" -> FAZ).

2 · RELATION
Label a relation only if the headline itself asserts or reports one of these between two ECONOMIC ACTORS:
  acquires      buys / takes over / agrees, plans, offers or nears a deal to buy a company, business unit or controlling stake; a takeover bid or approach counts.
  sells         sells / divests / exits / cuts a holding or unit, to someone or in some company. "X Stake Falls to 6%: Y" -> Y sells X.
  invests in    takes or raises a minority stake, invests capital, funds a round, or an insider buys shares. "X Stake Rises to 27%: Y" -> Y invests in X. "X 11.6% Stake Reported by Y" -> Y invests in X.
  supplies      supplies / ships / delivers goods or services to, or wins a supply contract from, the other actor.
  partners with signs a deal, agreement, partnership, JV, licensing or collaboration that is not an acquisition or investment.
  sues          files a lawsuit or legal claim against the other actor.
ECONOMIC ACTORS are companies, banks, investment firms and funds, sovereign wealth funds, and individuals acting as investors or dealmakers (e.g. Elon Musk buying Twitter). NOT actors: governments, countries, regulators, agencies, courts, central banks, politicians acting officially, news outlets.
Label NONE when the headline is: an analyst rating or price-target change (even with "Buy"/"Sell"); a bond, bill or note issue; a share buyback; a commodity tender; a proxy vote ("Backs X on 3 of 4 Proposals"); a purchase or sale of something that is not a company (bonds, buildings, power plants, apps, projects, gold) unless both parties are named economic actors — see the boundary rules; a deal whose counterparty is not named; a deal that is withdrawn, terminated, rejected, blocked or abandoned in this headline; a regulator or government action; macro or market-move news.
SUBJECTS = the actor performing the predicate (buyer, seller, investor, supplier, plaintiff). OBJECTS = the other economic actor. List every acceptable name when more than one fits: joint actors ("ADIA, GIC Nearing Stake" -> subjects [ADIA, GIC]); "Lukoil Sells Refinery ISAB to GOI" -> objects [ISAB, GOI]. For "partners with" either order is fine.
Words after a final colon are usually the SOURCE ("...: Bloomberg", "...: TOI"), except in stake filings ("X Stake Falls to 7%: Nomura Sec.") where they name the holder, who is the subject.
BOUNDARY RULES (v2 — each applies to every headline):
- A relation needs TWO NAMED economic actors that transact with each other. What changes hands does not matter: "Copper Road Sells Copper Road Project to Sterling Metals Corp." -> Copper Road sells to Sterling Metals Corp. But if either side is unnamed ("to European Spot Buyer", "Businesses for $50 Million", "a NYC Building", "New Shares" to the public), label NONE.
- Intentions count when the headline names both sides: "BAT Mulls Selling Stake" in ITC -> BAT sells ITC; "Mulls Options for UniCredit Stake" -> NONE (options are not a sale). Talks "on hold" -> NONE.
- A country, a government or "the US" is never an actor, even when a state company is meant ("Signs Agreement With Kuwait" -> NONE).
- When a headline states two relations, every one of them is an acceptable answer ("Ark Funds Buy Blade Air Mobility; Sell Verve Therapeutics" -> Ark invests in Blade Air Mobility OR Ark sells Verve Therapeutics).
Judge by the headline and its date only. Do not use knowledge of what happened afterwards.
